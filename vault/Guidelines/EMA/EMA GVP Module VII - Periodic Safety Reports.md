# EMA GVP Module VII — Periodic Safety Update Reports

## Overview

The Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Report (PBRER) — previously called the Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR) — is the primary periodic safety document submitted by Marketing Authorisation Holders (MAHs) to regulatory authorities worldwide. It provides an integrated benefit-risk evaluation of a medicinal product over a defined reporting period.

The PBRER follows the ICH E2C(R2) guideline and is harmonised across ICH regions (EU, US, Japan). In the EU, submission is to EMA via the electronic Common Technical Document (eCTD) or via national competent authorities.

## PBRER Structure (ICH E2C(R2) Format)

1. **Executive Summary** — benefit-risk conclusion
2. **Introduction** — product overview, regulatory history
3. **Worldwide Market Authorisation Status**
4. **Actions Taken for Safety Reasons During Reporting Period**
5. **Changes to Reference Safety Information (RSI)** — SmPC updates
6. **Estimated Exposure and Use Patterns**
   - Cumulative exposure (patient-years where available)
   - Exposure in special populations (pediatric, geriatric, pregnancy)
7. **Data in Summary Tabulations**
   - Cumulative summary tabulation of serious adverse events from clinical trials
   - Cumulative and interval summary tabulations from post-marketing experience
8. **Summaries of Significant Findings from Clinical Trials**
9. **Findings from Non-Interventional Studies**
10. **Information from Other Clinical Trials/Sources**
11. **Non-Clinical Data**
12. **Literature** — published case reports, epidemiology studies
13. **Other Periodic Reports** (where relevant)
14. **Lack of Efficacy** (where applicable)
15. **Late-Breaking Information** — significant findings after data lock
16. **Signal and Risk Evaluation**
    - Summary of signals detected and evaluated during reporting period
    - Status of ongoing safety concerns
    - Risk minimisation measures and effectiveness
17. **Benefit Evaluation** — current benefit data for approved indications
18. **Integrated Benefit-Risk Analysis for Approved Indications**
19. **Conclusions** — overall benefit-risk assessment, proposed actions
20. **Appendices** — line listings, literature, signal details

## Reporting Frequency and Data Lock Point

### EU PBRER Submission Schedule
Determined by the Data Lock Point (DLP) — the date after which no further data is included in the report.

**Standard frequencies**:
- **New products**: 6-monthly PSURs for 2 years post-approval, then annual for 2 years, then 3-yearly
- **Established products**: 3-yearly, or as specified in the EURD List (List of Union Reference Dates)
- **Products under additional monitoring**: May require more frequent reporting

**EURD List**: EMA publishes the Union Reference Date list specifying DLPs and submission frequencies for each active substance. All MAHs for the same substance submit on the same DLP cycle.

### Submission Timeline
- 60 days after DLP for 6-monthly/annual PSURs
- 90 days after DLP for 3-yearly PSURs (for products not on EURD list or as specified)

## Signal Evaluation in PBRER (Section 16)

Section 16 is the core PV section:

**16.1 Summary of safety signals during reporting period**
- All signals detected from any source
- Whether validated, prioritized, assessed
- Current status (ongoing, resolved, incorporated into SmPC)

**16.2 Signal evaluation**
For each validated signal evaluated during the period:
- Signal description and sources
- Clinical/epidemiological data review
- Causality assessment
- Regulatory action taken or proposed

**16.3 Characterisation of risks**
- Update on all ongoing safety concerns from RMP SVII/SVIII
- New data on identified and potential risks

**16.4 Effectiveness of risk minimisation**
- Assessment of additional risk minimisation measure outcomes (if applicable)

## Benefit-Risk Evaluation Framework

The benefit-risk evaluation in PBRER follows structured methodology:
1. **Context setting**: Disease severity, available alternatives, treatment goal
2. **Benefit assessment**: Efficacy data, patient-relevant outcomes, quality of life
3. **Risk assessment**: Safety profile update, quantification where possible
4. **Benefit-risk analysis**: Integrated qualitative assessment; EMA uses BRR (Benefit-Risk Ratio) framework
5. **Conclusion**: Positive/negative/uncertain benefit-risk for each indication

## Relationship to FDA Periodic Reporting

| Aspect | EU PBRER | US Periodic Adverse Drug Experience Report (PADER) |
|---|---|---|
| Format | ICH E2C(R2) harmonised | FDA-specific format (21 CFR §314.81) |
| Frequency | EURD list-driven (typically 3-yearly) | Quarterly (first 3 years), then annual |
| Content | Integrated benefit-risk | Primarily safety data summary |
| Scope | All indications, worldwide data | US-specific experience |
| Acceptance of ICH E2C(R2) | Required | Accepted (FDA allows PBRER in lieu of PADER) |

## GVP Module VII and Signal Management Integration

PBRER data lock point reviews should align with signal management timelines. Signals identified and assessed during the PBRER review period must be:
1. Documented in Section 16
2. Reflected in any SmPC updates (Section 5)
3. Incorporated into updated RMP if risk profile changes (submitted separately)

If a signal is urgent, it must not wait for the PBRER cycle — it requires immediate reporting per GVP Module IX procedures.
