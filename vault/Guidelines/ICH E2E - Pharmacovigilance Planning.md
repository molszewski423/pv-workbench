# ICH E2E — Pharmacovigilance Planning

## Purpose

ICH E2E provides guidance on pharmacovigilance planning at the time of submission for marketing authorization. It describes the safety specification and pharmacovigilance plan that form part of the Risk Management Plan (RMP).

## Structure of a Pharmacovigilance Plan

A pharmacovigilance plan has two core components:
1. **Safety Specification** — summary of important identified and potential risks, missing information
2. **Pharmacovigilance Plan** — activities to characterize and monitor identified safety issues

## Safety Specification Components

### Important Identified Risks
Risks for which there is adequate evidence of an association with the product:
- Adverse drug reactions established in clinical development
- Risks identified in post-marketing experience

### Important Potential Risks
Risks for which there is some basis for concern but insufficient evidence to confirm:
- Non-clinical findings suggesting potential harm
- Class effects from related compounds
- Signals that require further investigation

### Missing Information
Gaps in knowledge relevant to safety evaluation:
- Limited data in special populations (elderly, pediatric, renal/hepatic impairment)
- Long-term safety data
- Drug interactions not studied

## Pharmacovigilance Activities

### Routine Pharmacovigilance
Activities required for all products:
- ICSR collection, processing, and expedited reporting
- Signal detection from spontaneous reports and literature
- Preparation of periodic safety update reports (PSURs/PBRERs)
- Continuous benefit-risk monitoring

### Additional Pharmacovigilance
Product-specific activities addressing identified concerns:
- Post-authorization safety studies (PASS)
- Registries
- Enhanced pharmacovigilance programs
- Active surveillance

## Signal Management (ICH E2E-aligned)

The four-component signal management process:
1. **Signal Detection** — systematic review of safety databases, literature, and other sources to identify potential signals
2. **Signal Validation** — determine whether sufficient data exists to proceed with signal assessment
3. **Signal Analysis and Prioritization** — evaluate clinical importance, strength of evidence, public health impact
4. **Recommendation for Action** — decide appropriate regulatory or risk minimization response

Signal detection methods recommended by E2E:
- Case-by-case clinical review of spontaneous reports
- Disproportionality analysis (PRR, ROR, BCPNN, GPS)
- Clinical and epidemiological studies
- Literature review

## Quantitative Signal Detection Methods

### Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR)
- Compares reporting frequency of a drug-event pair against all other drugs
- Evans criteria: PRR ≥ 2, N ≥ 3, chi² ≥ 4 (see [[Evans Criteria PRR Signal Detection]])

### Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR)
- Analogous to case-control design using spontaneous report data
- Signal if lower bound of 95% CI > 1

### Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN)
- Used by WHO Uppsala Monitoring Centre (VigiBase)
- Information Component (IC) > 0 with lower CI > 0 = signal

### Gamma Poisson Shrinker (GPS)
- Used by FDA for FAERS analysis
- Empirical Bayes geometric mean (EBGM) ≥ 2 = signal

## Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR / PBRER)

Under ICH E2C(R2), marketing authorization holders must submit Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Reports (PBRERs, formerly PSURs) at defined intervals:
- First 2 years: every 6 months
- Years 2–10: annually
- After 10 years: every 3 years (may be modified by regulators)

PBRER content includes:
- Worldwide marketing authorization status
- Actions taken for safety reasons
- Changes to reference safety information
- Exposure data
- Individual case reports summary
- Signal and risk evaluation
- Overall benefit-risk evaluation

## Literature Monitoring Requirements

Sponsors must conduct systematic literature review to identify:
- Published case reports of adverse events
- Epidemiological studies reporting safety findings
- Signal-generating publications

Minimum frequency: continuous monitoring, with weekly review for key sources (MEDLINE, EMBASE). Findings meeting SUSAR criteria require expedited reporting under ICH E2A.

## Risk Management Plan (RMP)

The RMP integrates the safety specification and pharmacovigilance plan. EMA requires an RMP for all new marketing authorizations. Key sections:
- Part I: Product overview
- Part II: Safety specification
- Part III: Pharmacovigilance plan
- Part IV: Plans for post-authorization efficacy studies
- Part V: Risk minimization measures
- Part VI: Summary
