# ICH E2A — Clinical Safety Data Management

## Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)

In clinical trials, all noxious and unintended responses to a medicinal product related to any dose are considered adverse drug reactions where a causal relationship between the medicinal product and an adverse event is at least a reasonable possibility.

In post-marketing, an ADR refers to a noxious and unintended response at normally used doses.

## Serious Adverse Event or Reaction — Criteria

A serious adverse event (SAE) or serious adverse drug reaction is any untoward medical occurrence that at any dose:

1. **Results in death**
2. **Is life-threatening** — the patient is at immediate risk of death at the time of the reaction (does not include reactions that hypothetically might have caused death if more severe)
3. **Requires inpatient hospitalization** or prolongation of existing hospitalization
4. **Results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity**
5. **Is a congenital anomaly or birth defect**
6. **Is a medically important event** — may not be immediately life-threatening or fatal but may jeopardize the patient or may require intervention to prevent one of the above outcomes

Medical and scientific judgment should be applied in deciding whether an event is medically important.

## Valid ICSR — Minimum Criteria

A minimum valid Individual Case Safety Report requires all four elements:
1. An **identifiable patient** (initials, age, sex, date of birth, or other identifier)
2. A **suspect medicinal product** (name, dose, route)
3. A **suspect adverse reaction** (description)
4. An **identifiable reporter** (name, address, or other identifier)

A case without all four elements is not processable and must be followed up.

## Unexpected Adverse Drug Reaction

An adverse reaction whose **nature, severity, specificity, or outcome** is not consistent with the applicable product information:
- For unapproved investigational products: Investigator's Brochure (IB)
- For approved products: Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) or equivalent

The term "unexpected" refers to an event not previously observed, not merely an event that might have been anticipated.

## Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction (SUSAR)

A SUSAR meets all three criteria:
- **Serious** — meets at least one E2A seriousness criterion
- **Unexpected** — not consistent with applicable product information
- **Causally related** — reasonable possibility of causal relationship to the investigational medicinal product

## Expedited Reporting Timelines

| Event Type | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Fatal or life-threatening **unexpected** SUSAR | **7 calendar days** (initial); 8 additional days for follow-up |
| All other (non-fatal/non-life-threatening) unexpected SUSARs | **15 calendar days** |
| Serious expected ADRs | Included in periodic reports (IND annual report / DSUR) |
| Non-serious unexpected ADRs in clinical trials | Included in Annual Safety Report |

The 7-day clock starts from the date the sponsor first receives minimum valid information.

## Causality Assessment

A **reasonable possibility** of causal relationship is the threshold for regulatory reporting. The sponsor should not substitute their causality judgment for the reporter's assessment — if the reporter considers a relationship possible, it should be reported.

Causality terms used:
- **Related / Possibly related / Probably related** → report
- **Unrelated / Unlikely related** → report if serious and unexpected in clinical trials

## De-challenge and Re-challenge

- **De-challenge**: Withdrawal of the suspect drug; document whether adverse event resolved, improved, or persisted
- **Positive de-challenge**: Event resolved or improved after drug withdrawal
- **Re-challenge**: Reintroduction of the drug; positive re-challenge (event recurs) strengthens causality

## Narrative Requirements

The clinical narrative should include:
- Patient demographics and relevant medical history
- Suspect drug(s): dose, route, indication, dates
- Concomitant medications with dates
- Adverse event: onset, description, laboratory findings, clinical course
- Treatment of adverse event and outcome
- Reporter's causality assessment
- De-challenge and re-challenge information if available
