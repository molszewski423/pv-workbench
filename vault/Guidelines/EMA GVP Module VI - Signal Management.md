# EMA GVP Module VI — Signal Management

## Definitions

### Signal
A signal is **information arising from one or multiple sources, including observations and experiments**, which suggests a new potentially causal association, or a new aspect of a known association, between an intervention and an event or set of related events, either adverse or beneficial, that is **judged to be of sufficient likelihood to justify verificatory action**.

### Signal Detection
Signal detection is the process of identifying signals from data obtained from spontaneous reporting systems, clinical studies, scientific literature, or other sources.

### Signal Validation
Signal validation is the process of evaluating the data underlying a potential signal to verify that the **available documentation contains sufficient evidence to suggest a new potentially causal association or a new aspect of a known association** and therefore justifies further evaluation.

### Signal Analysis and Prioritization
Assessment of validated signals to determine their **clinical importance, strength of evidence, and public health impact**, and to prioritize them for regulatory action.

## Signal Management Process — Four Components

1. **Signal detection** — systematic review of safety data to identify potential signals using quantitative (disproportionality) and qualitative methods
2. **Signal validation** — verify sufficient evidence exists to proceed with formal assessment
3. **Signal analysis and prioritization** — evaluate clinical relevance, assess benefit-risk implications, determine urgency
4. **Recommendation for action** — conclude on appropriate regulatory response (e.g., labeling update, DHPC, referral, suspension)

## Data Sources for Signal Detection

- Spontaneous adverse drug reaction reports (EudraVigilance, FAERS, VigiBase)
- Clinical study data (randomized controlled trials, observational studies)
- Post-authorization safety studies (PASS)
- Scientific literature (MEDLINE, EMBASE, national literature)
- Patient registries
- Digital health data and real-world evidence
- Company-sponsored compassionate use / named patient programs

## EudraVigilance and Signal Detection

EMA performs signal detection in EudraVigilance (EU spontaneous reporting database) using:
- Quantitative detection: disproportionality analysis (PRR, ROR, IC)
- Qualitative review: Medical review of individual case series
- Minimum threshold before quantitative analysis: typically N ≥ 3

Marketing authorization holders (MAHs) are required to monitor EudraVigilance for signals relevant to their products.

## Reporting Timelines

| Event | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Fatal or life-threatening unexpected SUSAR | **7 calendar days** from awareness |
| All other unexpected SUSARs | **15 calendar days** from awareness |
| Serious ADRs from literature | 15 calendar days from article identification |
| Non-serious unexpected ADRs | Included in PSUR/PBRER |

## Literature Monitoring

MAHs must conduct **systematic and ongoing literature review** to identify adverse drug reactions reported in scientific and medical literature:
- **Minimum sources**: MEDLINE and EMBASE (or equivalent)
- **Minimum frequency**: Weekly review of key sources; additional review as needed
- Published case reports meeting SUSAR criteria require expedited reporting under ICH E2A timelines
- Literature review findings must be included in the PSUR/PBRER

## Signal Evaluation and the PRAC

The EMA's Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee (PRAC) is responsible for:
- Evaluating signals detected in EudraVigilance
- Recommending regulatory actions to the CHMP and CMDh
- Reviewing PSURs/PBRERs
- Assessing post-authorization safety studies (PASS)

PRAC signal assessment results are published monthly on the EMA website.

## Regulatory Actions Following Signal Assessment

Depending on signal strength and clinical importance:
- **No action** — insufficient evidence or signal refuted
- **Labeling update** — update SmPC/PIL to add warning, contraindication, or new ADR
- **Direct Healthcare Professional Communication (DHPC)** — urgent safety communication
- **Risk minimization measures** — additional educational materials, restricted prescribing
- **Referral** — Article 20 or Article 31 procedure for safety review
- **Suspension or withdrawal** — if benefit-risk becomes unfavorable

## Marketing Authorization Holder Responsibilities

MAHs must:
1. Operate a Pharmacovigilance System (described in PSMF — Pharmacovigilance System Master File)
2. Appoint a Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV) with EU residency
3. Report ICSRs to EudraVigilance within required timelines
4. Submit PSURs/PBRERs at required intervals
5. Conduct and submit PASS when required
6. Maintain the Risk Management Plan (RMP)
7. Monitor signals proactively, not only reactively

## Signal Workflow in Practice

```
Data Sources → Signal Detection → Validation → Analysis → 
PRAC/MAH Review → Recommendation → Regulatory Action → 
SmPC Update / Communication → Monitoring for Effect
```
